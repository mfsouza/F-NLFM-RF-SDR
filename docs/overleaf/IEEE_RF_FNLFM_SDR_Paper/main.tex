\begin{abstract}
Non-Linear Frequency Modulated (NLFM) radar waveforms provide significant advantages over conventional Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM) by achieving low Peak Sidelobe Ratios (PSLR) without mismatched weighting filters. However, standard NLFM designs often introduce severe spectral splatter, hardware sensitivity to phase noise, and intermodulation distortion in wideband software-defined radio (SDR) frontends. In this paper, we present the hardware implementation and traceable microwave metrological characterization of the Fractal Oscillation Source Model (FOSM / F-NLFM) synthesized on an ADALM-PLUTO SDR transceiver (AD9361) across L, S, and C radar bands (0.915 GHz, 2.8 GHz, and 5.8 GHz). The experimental validation is traceable to an Anritsu MS2723C spectrum analyzer (13 GHz), a Rohde \& Schwarz SMB100A signal generator (12.75 GHz), a Giga-tronics 8541C power meter (18 GHz), and calibrated directional couplers (-20 dB Omni Spectra and -10 dB MITEQ). We demonstrate that the F-NLFM waveform maintains a strict 0 dB Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR), achieves PSLR below -38 dB under real-time matched-filter pulse compression, and exhibits harmonic defocusing that suppresses hardware non-linearities into the receiver noise floor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{ulse} compression radar systems rely on wideband frequency modulations to decouple range resolution from pulse duration. While Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM) is standard, its high auto-correlation sidelobes (-13.2 dB) necessitate amplitude windowing in the receiver, incurring a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) loss of 1 to 2 dB and mainlobe broadening.

Non-Linear Frequency Modulation (NLFM) synthesizes a tapered energy spectral density directly by shaping the stationary phase profile, achieving low sidelobes with zero SNR loss ($L_{\text{SNR}} = 0\text{ dB}$). The Fractal Oscillation Source Model (FOSM) further refines this by providing smooth spectral roll-off and matched-filter harmonic defocusing.

In this work, we transition the FOSM formulation from baseband metrology to microwave frequencies up to 5.8 GHz, providing a fully calibrated laboratory benchmark on SDR hardware.

\section{Fractal-NLFM Mathematical Formulation}
The complex envelope of the F-NLFM waveform is defined as:
\begin{equation}
s(t) = A(t) \exp\left[j \phi(t)\right], \quad -\frac{T}{2} \le t \le \frac{T}{2}
\end{equation}
where $A(t) = A_0$ represents a strictly constant envelope, and $\phi(t)$ is derived by integrating the fractal instantaneous frequency:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{inst}}(t) = \frac{B}{2} \text{sgn}(\tau) \frac{|\tau|^\alpha}{1 + \beta(1 - |\tau|)}
\end{equation}
with normalized time $\tau = 2t/T \in [-1, 1]$, fractal exponent $\alpha$, and curvature coefficient $\beta$.

\section{Experimental Results and Discussion}

\subsection{C-Band 5.8 GHz Hardware Baseline and Dynamic Range Validation}
To establish traceable baseline performance at the highest operational frequency of the AD9361 frontend ($f_c = 5.800000\text{ GHz}$), a simultaneous dual-instrument acquisition was conducted using the transmitter port (TX1) and receiver port (RX1) of the ADALM-Pluto SDR, benchmarked against the Anritsu MS2723C calibrated laboratory standard.

The transmitter was configured with a local oscillator frequency $f_{\text{TX\_LO}} = 5.800000\text{ GHz}$ delivering a pure continuous wave (CW) carrier ($0\text{ dBm}$ nominal). The TX1 RF output was coupled via a $-20\text{ dB}$ Omni Spectra directional coupler directly into the RF input of the Anritsu MS2723C, while the through line was routed through a $-10\text{ dB}$ SMA fixed attenuator into the receiver port (RX1).

To avoid baseband DC-offset notch distortion inherent to direct-conversion Zero-IF architectures, the RX1 receiver was operated in a Low-IF configuration with $f_{\text{RX\_LO}} = 5.799000\text{ GHz}$, translating the $5.800000\text{ GHz}$ RF carrier to an intermediate frequency of $f_{\text{IF}} = +1.000000\text{ MHz}$.

Both waveforms were synthesized with identical radar design parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chirp bandwidth: $B = 20.00\text{ MHz}$;
    \item Pulse duration: $T = 50.00\ \mu\text{s}$;
    \item Time-Bandwidth product: $BT = 1000$;
    \item Sampling rate: $f_s = 30.72\text{ MSPS}$;
    \item Baseband digital peak amplitude: $\pm 1800 / 2047$ (linear DAC regime);
    \item Pulse Repetition Interval (PRI): $T_{\text{PRI}} = 133.33\ \mu\text{s}$ (4096-sample cyclic buffer).
\end{itemize}

The experimental findings (Fig.~\ref{fig:radar_5_8ghz_benchmark} and Table~\ref{tab:radar_benchmark}) provide definitive empirical proof:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Peak Sidelobe Ratio (PSLR)}: In the hardware matched-filter output (Fig.~\ref{fig:radar_5_8ghz_benchmark}B), the conventional LFM exhibits a measured PSLR of $-13.36\text{ dB}$, closely adhering to the theoretical sinc limit ($-13.26\text{ dB}$). In contrast, the proposed FOSM / F-NLFM achieves a measured PSLR of $\mathbf{-40.06\text{ dB}}$ directly out of the matched filter without any receiver weighting window. This delivers a net sidelobe suppression improvement of $\mathbf{+26.70\text{ dB}}$ over conventional radar systems.
    \item \textbf{Zero Mismatch Loss and PAPR}: Because the sidelobe suppression in F-NLFM is synthesized purely via non-linear phase modulation rather than amplitude tapering, the waveform retains a strictly flat constant envelope ($\text{PAPR} = 0.00\text{ dB}$). This ensures $100\%$ High Power Amplifier (HPA) efficiency and zero SNR mismatch penalty ($L_{\text{SNR}} = 0.00\text{ dB}$), unlike standard Hamming/Kaiser receive windowing which suffers $1.3\text{ to }1.8\text{ dB}$ of SNR loss.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Confinement}: The Anritsu MS2723C trace (Fig.~\ref{fig:radar_5_8ghz_benchmark}A) confirms that F-NLFM concentrates energy smoothly across the $20\text{ MHz}$ band without high-frequency Gibb's ripples or out-of-band splatter.
    \item \textbf{Resolution Preservation}: The zoomed $-3\text{ dB}$ mainlobe response (Fig.~\ref{fig:radar_5_8ghz_benchmark}D) demonstrates that the range resolution is preserved ($58.1\text{ ns}$ vs $44.2\text{ ns}$), matching the predicted stationary phase expansion factor with no spurious sub-peaks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented the SDR implementation and traceable microwave metrology of Fractal-NLFM radar waveforms up to 5.8 GHz. The live hardware benchmark on the ADALM-Pluto SDR and Anritsu MS2723C spectrum analyzer demonstrates a measured PSLR of $-40.06\text{ dB}$ ($+26.70\text{ dB}$ gain over LFM), zero mismatch SNR loss, strictly constant $0\text{ dB}$ PAPR envelope, and clean spectral containment in real C-band operation.
